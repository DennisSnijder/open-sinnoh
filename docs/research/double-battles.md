Research date: 2026-10-15. **Proposal only; no runtime or contract changes in this PR.**

Primary reference: pret/pokeplatinum at [`1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20`](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/tree/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20). ## 1. Battle types, entry points and trainer data

`BATTLE_TYPE_TRAINER=1`, `DOUBLES=2`, `LINK=4`, `2vs2=8`, `TAG=16`, `AI=64`. The engine's `TAG` composite is **the player alone facing two separate enemy trainers** (despite the ambiguous flag comment); `2vs2|AI` means a separate AI owner for the player's second position. Concrete offline combinations: ordinary trainer double `3`; two enemy trainers vs one player `19`; partner wild `74`; partner trainer `75`. Doubles also force set mode; `AI` is included in the no-AI-items mask. [include/constants/battle.h:24–56](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/include/constants/battle.h#L24-L56) [src/encounter.c:724–757](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/encounter.c#L724-L757)

| Offline topology | Composite / bits | Numeric mask |
|---|---|---|
| one trainer, two active Pokémon per side | `BATTLE_TYPE_TRAINER_DOUBLES = TRAINER \| DOUBLES` | 3 |
| player commands two, two independent enemy trainers | `BATTLE_TYPE_TAG_DOUBLES = TRAINER \| DOUBLES \| TAG` | 19 |
| player + AI partner against two wild Pokémon | `BATTLE_TYPE_AI_PARTNER = DOUBLES \| 2vs2 \| AI` | 74 |
| player + AI partner against two trainers | `BATTLE_TYPE_TRAINER_WITH_AI_PARTNER = TRAINER \| DOUBLES \| 2vs2 \| AI` | 75 |

These masks follow the [flag definitions, include/constants/battle.h:24–55](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/include/constants/battle.h#L24-L55) and [encounter constructor, src/encounter.c:730–755](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/encounter.c#L730-L755).

`ScrCmd_StartTrainerBattle` reads enemy trainer IDs 1 and 2 and obtains an optional partner from the persistent has-partner flag and `VAR_PARTNER_TRAINER_ID`. `ScrCmd_StartTagBattle` instead reads partner ID explicitly first, then the two enemies. Both call the same constructor. Distinct nonzero enemies select `19` without partner / `75` with partner; equal IDs select `3`; otherwise trainer singles `1`. Important edge: passing a partner does **not** by itself make a one-enemy trainer fight multi; the constructor's enemy-ID branches decide. [src/scrcmd_trainer.c:124–161](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/scrcmd_trainer.c#L124-L161) [src/encounter.c:724–757](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/encounter.c#L724-L757)

Trainer doubles are data, not a trainer-class inference: `.double_battle` produces the header's doubles bit and `.ai_flags` produces its AI mask. Loading the trainer encounter builds a party for each nonzero owner ID and ORs the final loaded header's battle type into the encounter. For an ordinary double the same trainer ID can occupy both enemy positions; this is one shared trainer party conceptually, distinct from two independent trainer IDs. Example `twins_liv_and_liz.json`: class `TRAINER_CLASS_TWINS`, doubles true, two Pokémon, BASIC+EXPERT. [tools/dataproc/src/trainerproc.c:141–164](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/tools/dataproc/src/trainerproc.c#L141-L164) [src/trainer_data.c:25–51](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/trainer_data.c#L25-L51) [res/trainers/data/twins_liv_and_liz.json:1–29](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/res/trainers/data/twins_liv_and_liz.json#L1-L29)

Sight fights distinguish a double-trainer record from two unrelated trainers seeing the player together. With two healthy player Pokémon, two independent sight hits become `APPROACH_TYPE_VS2`; with a double record, the field finds the other map object with the same trainer ID and uses `APPROACH_TYPE_DOUBLES`. The latter is refused on sight if the player lacks two living Pokémon. Script IDs in the single and double ranges map back to one trainer ID; their range identifies the dialogue half, while the trainer header answers whether it is a double record. The common script passes the two discovered trainer IDs to `StartTrainerBattle` in either case, which is why equal/distinct IDs matter. [src/trainer_encounter.c:84–125](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/trainer_encounter.c#L84-L125) [src/trainer_encounter.c:356–374](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/trainer_encounter.c#L356-L374) [src/script_manager.c:498–515](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/script_manager.c#L498-L515) [res/field/scripts/scripts_battles.s:125–178](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/res/field/scripts/scripts_battles.s#L125-L178) [res/field/scripts/scripts_battles.s:202–218](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/res/field/scripts/scripts_battles.s#L202-L218)

The binary trainer header separates class, party size, four trainer items, AI mask and battle-type bits. Its monster-data type selects base records, custom moves, held items, or both; this is independent of the doubles flag. There is no separate “trainer pair” team record required by the encounter constructor: two trainer IDs supply two headers and two rosters. [include/struct_defs/trainer_data.h:14–64](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/include/struct_defs/trainer_data.h#L14-L64), [src/encounter.c:730–755](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/encounter.c#L730-L755).

At runtime ordinary doubles route both friendly slots to the same side party; `2vs2` routes each battler to its own party, while TAG does that for the enemies only. This is the direct ownership evidence behind the topology distinction. [src/battle/battle_system.c:124–156](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_system.c#L124-L156).

## 2. Commands, targets and turn resolution

The controller walks battlers in numeric order; a shared controller defers its second slot until the first slot reaches command-selection end. Thus a player commanding two Pokémon makes slot-one's choice and then slot-two's, whereas a partner-controlled slot has its own AI task. When the second-slot menu exposes Cancel, cancelling resets both choices; a committed first item can disable that rewind (details below). This is selection order, not execution order. [command state machine](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_controller_player.c#L307-L345), [cancel](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_controller_player.c#L486-L495), [AI dispatch](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_display.c#L5630-L5643).

Execution first prioritizes running, then places items/switches ahead of attacks, then compares battlers choosing the same command by speed (including move priority only for Fight). Items and switches share the initial bucket but do not compare speed against one another unless command types match: preserve this distinction in parity tests instead of assuming one generic switch priority. [turn ordering](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_controller_player.c#L707-L787).

After a valid move choice the DS enters target selection in doubles, including a confirmation screen for moves with automatic/group ranges; `RANGE_ALLY` skips it when the partner is absent. Non-Ghost Curse uses user targeting. The distinction between the confirmation highlight and the execution target matters: Counter/Mirror Coat and random-opponent moves use the user-shaped confirmation layout but resolve targets separately. [selection decision](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_controller_player.c#L4342-L4369), [layout mapping](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_subscreen.c#L1336-L1359), [execution resolution](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_lib.c#L1651-L1708).

The DS screen uses four Pokémon positions (enemy pair on the top row, friendly pair on the next, cancel on the bottom); single-target layouts move a cursor among eligible slots, whereas group layouts use a combined cursor/highlight. Presence is checked separately from each layout's eligibility mask. The following table translates the source's masks/layouts into semantic targets. [button layout](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_subscreen.c#L374-L378), [masks](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_subscreen.c#L797-L810), [range-to-layout](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_subscreen.c#L1336-L1359), [presence/highlight](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_subscreen.c#L2567-L2603), [cursor behavior](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_subscreen.c#L3402-L3434).

| --- | --- |

| `RANGE_SINGLE_TARGET_ME_FIRST` | Choose either enemy. |

Spread targeting starts with the first living eligible battler in speed order; the adjacent-opponents range excludes allies, and all-adjacent excludes only the user. Damage is multiplied by 3/4 for adjacent-opponents when two battlers on the defending side remain alive, and for all-adjacent when at least two living battlers other than the current defender exist. These source conditions deserve tests for absent/immune/fainting targets instead of assuming the reduction follows menu highlights. [spread resolution](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_lib.c#L1611-L1650), [damage reduction](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_lib.c#L7035-L7043), [count semantics](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_lib.c#L2825-L2848).

At execution a normal selected target still alive is retained; otherwise the routine attempts a living random opponent. Follow Me can redirect normal and random-opponent attacks. User-or-ally falls back to self if the chosen ally has fainted. These are execution-time rules, independent of whether the target screen originally accepted a slot. [target resolution](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_lib.c#L1676-L1725).

Faint messages are processed in speed order. Replacement differs by ownership: shared-party doubles (and the player's side against a trainer pair) reserve the surviving active slot, replace slot one first when both faint, and leave an empty slot when no eligible reserve remains; independent-owner sides examine each owner's party. Doubles force set mode, so the singles optional shift prompt is not used. Crucially, a story AI-partner battle is lost when the player's own party runs out, even if the AI partner still has Pokémon. [faint order](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_controller_player.c#L4409-L4420), [replacement](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_controller_player.c#L4070-L4161), [forced-set mask](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/include/constants/battle.h#L50), [player loss rule](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_controller_player.c#L4189-L4209).

Switch selection checks trapping and passes the first slot's already-selected replacement to the second slot's party menu, preventing both slots choosing the same reserve. A selected item is stored per battler action; the recovery-item party menu uses the shared owner in ordinary doubles and the individual owner in multi. Running is rejected in ordinary trainer battles using bank 368 message 793 `BattleStrings_Text_NoTheresNoRunningFromATrainerBattle`; in non-multi doubles a valid Run choice also ends the partner's command selection. [switch/item command collection](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_controller_player.c#L555-L649), [item ownership menu](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_display.c#L3877-L3907). The item commit/cancel boundary is described below; do not treat every first-slot action as rewindable.

For wild AI-partner doubles, the bag rejects a ball while both enemies remain present (`BattleBag_Text_CantUseBallTwoPokemon`); once one is absent it checks the survivor's semi-invulnerable/substitute state. The catch task chooses enemy slot one unless absent, then slot two, and uses double-position throw types 16/17 rather than singles type 15. ## 3. Partner and enemy AI

The AI flag routes every battler except battler zero through trainer command/move/target/party handlers. The move handler invokes trainer AI for trainer battles and for a friendly AI-controlled battler even in wild encounters; ordinary wild opponents instead choose among valid moves randomly. Thus the story partner uses the same trainer-AI engine, with its own trainer header's `aiMask`, rather than a separate partner-only policy. All doubles additionally enable `AI_FLAG_TAG_STRATEGY`. [task routing](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_display.c#L5630-L5643), [move routing](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/battle_display.c#L3580-L3616), [AI initialization](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/trainer_ai/trainer_ai.c#L223-L255).

Doubles AI evaluates every other living battler as a potential target, including its ally, reinitializing move scores and damage-roll estimates for each. For each target it randomly picks among best-scoring moves; ally candidates scoring below 100 are discarded. It then randomly picks among targets with the highest best score. Therefore move and target selection are coupled and consume random draws per candidate; running the existing singles scorer twice and picking an enemy is insufficient. The final routine also overrides non-Ghost Curse to self and contains a side-sensitive user-or-ally override that should be ported/tested exactly rather than generalized from its label. [candidate loop](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/trainer_ai/trainer_ai.c#L352-L438), [cross-target choice and overrides](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/trainer_ai/trainer_ai.c#L440-L471), [fresh damage draws](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/trainer_ai/trainer_ai.c#L233-L240).

The doubles strategy includes effectiveness/KO checks, a highest-damage comparison including the attacker's partner, move-specific weather/Trick Room/Follow Me handling, and ally damage/absorption checks. The basic routine explicitly exits when evaluating an ally target; the tag strategy supplies those ally-specific rules. Examples include penalizing harmful ally attacks while allowing Flash Fire activation and evaluating special support moves. This applies to enemy teams as well as the player's AI partner. [basic ally exit](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/trainer_ai/script.s#L52-L54), [tag main/damage and move-specific dispatch](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/trainer_ai/script.s#L6637-L6752), [ally checks](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/trainer_ai/script.s#L7339-L7362), [support examples](https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum/blob/1dc13dcea40c04ab53f1f607de46023dc409ef20/src/battle/trainer_ai/script.s#L7621-L7689). **Uncertain:** this is not an exhaustive audit of every tag-AI branch; preserve the entire interpreter opcode coverage and add branch fixtures when implementation starts.

### 5.1 Format, requests, choices and target locations

Use a custom format with `mod: 'gen4'`, `gameType: 'doubles'`, and an empty ruleset (or the existing ROM-backed gen4os mod with that game type). This creates two sides, each with two active positions. `gameType: 'multi'` creates four sides, each with one active; p1+p3 are allies and p2+p4 are allies. Native multi shares side conditions between allies, while keeping owner parties separate. These are mechanical formats, not VGC rules: do not add team preview, level normalization, species/item clauses or competitive bans. [Format/player count][S-format], [active slots][S-active], [alliance setup][S-alliance].

A move request has `active[]` in active-position order and `side.pokemon` for that owner's current simulator party order. Each active entry lists moves and their target type. Forced replacement is a separate request with `forceSwitch[]`; sides that need no input receive a wait request. Multi may also expose an ally's request data. The controller should wait for all required human/AI decisions before submitting the last choice that advances the sim. [Requests][S-requests], [choice parsing][S-choose].

Original API examples (choice strings only, not decomp code):

| Situation | `choose` side and choice string |
|---|---|
| doubles: first active uses move 1 on enemy position 1; second uses move 2 on enemy position 2 | p1: `move 1 1, move 2 2` |
| second active uses Helping Hand on first | p1: `move 1 1, move 3 -1` (with Helping Hand in move slot 3) |
| first switches to current simulator party position 3; second uses Surf | p1: `switch 3, move 2` (with Surf in move slot 2) |
| multi: player and partner choose independently | p1: `move 1 2`; p3: `move 1 1` |
| doubles: only the second position needs replacement | reply according to `forceSwitch[]`, e.g. `pass, switch 3`; `pass` is for a position with no required action |

Moves and switch choices use **one-based simulator indices**; move indices belong to the active's move slots, switch indices to the simulator's currently reordered party. They are not our persistent zero-based party indices. A comma joins the two actives' choices in doubles; it does not join different trainers' multi choices. [Move selection][S-move], [switch selection][S-switch], [parser][S-choose], [pass semantics][S-pass].

Target numbers are relative to the acting half, **not to the acting Pokémon**: positive 1/2 are the opposing half's first/second positions; negative -1/-2 are the friendly half's first/second positions. Thus -1 is self for the first active and ally for the second. In multi, the second friendly position belongs to p3 and the second enemy position to p4. Use `getLocOf`/`getAtLoc`, not `target ^ 1` or screen-left arithmetic. Explicit locations are required for selectable targets in doubles; group/self moves reject an explicit target location. [Location conversion][S-locations], [validation][S-target-validation], [choice validation][S-choice-target].

Measured with the installed package, Gen 4, seed `[1,2,3,4]`, original hand-built level-30 teams (no imported content): doubles returned `p1a,p1b,p2a,p2b`, two `active` request entries per side, accepted the Helping Hand and switch/Surf choices above, and reached turn 3. Multi returned **`p1a,p2a,p3b,p4b`**, one active request entry per owner, accepted four individual targeted choices and reached turn 2. The second multi owner's protocol suffix is `b`, even though its `active[0]` is its only active. The source's position offset explains this. These are API smoke probes, not a claim of full battle parity. [Slot offsets][S-slot], [location conversion][S-locations].

## 6. Proposed controller and contract changes

This section proposes implementation work; it does not ratify new contracts. Contract edits are a separate reviewed step.

### 6.1 Keep the seam, represent ownership

Keep `createBattle`, the 30 Hz generator timeline, immutable setup/seed plus choices, `Battle.step/view/done`, game-owned app hosting, the ROM-backed dex, text-bank messages, and battle result application. The present controller explicitly rejects multiple trainers, double trainers and multiple wild opponents; its one-active `Team` and prefix-based protocol routing are the places to replace, rather than adding a second copy of the singles controller. [Constructor][L-constructor], [team/action types][L-team], [routing][L-routing], [contracts][L-contract].

Proposed normalized setup:

| Concept | Proposed representation and invariant |
|---|---|
| Battle purpose | Retain `wild / trainer / first / tutorial`; add an orthogonal topology `single / double / trainer-pair / ai-partner`. No explosion of purpose strings such as “Riley trainer double”. |
| DS flags | Carry the validated decomp battle-type mask for AI/rules, derived once at the VM/world boundary. Reject contradictory topology/flags rather than guessing. |
| Owners | Local player, optional partner trainer ID, one or two enemy trainer IDs, or generated wild owners. Each owner has its own party, items and AI mask. Same trainer ID passed twice identifies one owner, not two clones. |
| Four positions | Stable battler IDs 0/1/2/3, explicit owner and party-slot binding, occupancy (`null` when absent). Side remains battler ID parity. Owner and battler slot never stand in for one another. |
| Persistent identity | `(owner, original party slot)` maps to simulator Pokémon identity; a separate mapping converts current simulator party indices at each request. Never persist a sim switch index. |
| Partner lifecycle | Use explicit script partner or the walking-partner state to build the battle; capture whether the persistent has-partner flag requires healing afterwards. Never infer this solely from the partner's class or follower sprite. |

Start with singles and ordinary doubles on native `doubles` (singles keeps its existing format); use native `multi` for four-position AI-partner wild/trainer battles (types 74/75). A wild multi's p2 and p4 each own their generated wild monster; the partner owns p3. Use the asymmetric adapter described in §5 for type 19. This choice uses native independent parties where they fit and isolates the one topology the sim cannot directly model.

Before committing to that asymmetric adapter, prove forced-switch filtering and party-scanning effects in a scratch fixture. If hooks cannot preserve ownership, revisit representation in the design review; do not duplicate the player's party across p1/p3 or allow an enemy to use both reserves. The combined enemy roster may contain twelve monsters (two legal six-member owner parties); validate each owner separately and allow the combined sim roster to retain every member. The sim builds all supplied members and caps its roster at 24, but our DS owner limit remains six. Add an explicit 6+6 fixture selecting the final reserve. [Sim roster construction][S-party], [capacity][S-capacity]. The implementation should fail loudly on internal identity mismatches.

### 6.2 Contract delta to propose

- **`BattleSetup` / `BattleRequest`:** add the normalized topology, partner owner and raw DS flags, with explicit first/second enemy identity. Existing `trainers[]` already carries slot order, but cannot express a friendly owner alone; do not overload it to mean both teams. Keep first/tutorial single constraints.
- **`BattleChoice`:** identify the acting battler on move/item/switch/run commands; a move carries an optional stable target battler, separate from its move slot. UI/replay targets must never expose simulator location numbers. Keep learning/naming/shift answers distinct from turn commands.
- **`BattlePrompt`:** add acting battler, whether the previous command can be rewound, and a target prompt with `selectable[]`, `highlighted[]`, cursor and confirmation mode. Target eligibility must combine ROM range, occupancy and contextual rules such as non-Ghost Curse. Show group confirmation even though no targetLoc is sent to the sim.
- **`BattleView`:** add stable battler IDs, owner and position to entries in the existing `battlers[]` view (two or four); add trainer-owner views and per-slot ball/send-out state. Keep side-wide background/platform/message state. Animation attacker/defender references become battler IDs, plus a target list for spread presentation; inspect `anim/` for two-side assumptions before changing contracts.
- **Party/bag app requests:** carry actor owner and excluded/reserved slots. Human selection remains confined to the player's party; a partner's faint never opens the player's party menu. Preserve move-specific item targets.
- **`BattleResult`:** keep the player's party/bag/money/caught result in original order. Extend seen/order accounting if necessary to include every foe send-out; don't return the partner roster as player-owned. Prefer a transient owner map over a saved battle schema because current battles cannot be saved in progress.
- **Replay grammar:** add actor and target addressing while retaining existing singles replay behavior; record canonical actions after local validation. Version contracts/replay grammar where necessary and apply the changes as one reviewed step.

These deltas extend currently single-actor choices/prompts and the unkeyed battler array; the sim continues to resolve moves. [Current seam, setup, choices and prompts][L-contract], [current views][L-view], [current protocol routing][L-routing].

### 6.3 Turn state and integration sequence

Proposed turn loop:

1. Establish which four-slot positions are occupied, locked/recharging, human-controlled or AI-controlled. Collect the player's first then second command where applicable. Rewind both only when the DS menu exposes second-slot Cancel, releasing uncommitted switch reservations. A committed first item disables that rewind.
2. Validate targets, switch ownership and inventory at the input boundary. Apply successful player menu-item effects/inventory immediately and synchronize active/party state before the next prompt or AI view. Freeze remaining choices before turn resolution. Have each AI owner use the existing scorer with the correct battler ID and owner party; preserve target and AI trace. Wild foes keep random valid-move selection.
3. Resolve remaining out-of-sim commands at the DS action boundary; do not reapply player items already committed in menus. Successful escape/catch ends battle and cancels remaining actions; failed escape/catch leaves the appropriate enemy/partner actions. For ordinary doubles, a side-wide Run consumes both selections. Handle AI-item/switch ordering and item action bookkeeping through the queue adaptation in §5.
4. Translate the frozen action vector once into simulator requests. Drain protocol into slot-addressed events; synchronize HP/PP/status/items for every owner binding. Render one spread animation followed by its per-target results in DS order, with separate animation support for scripts that visit targets.
5. On KOs, award EXP per defeated opponent using that opponent's participant mask. Collect replacements by DS slot/owner rules without a shift offer. Reserve the surviving active and any already-selected reserve. Resume the sim only when its entire required replacement request is satisfied.
6. Apply result, prize/penalty, player writeback and field healing. Return to the VM so its existing win/loss/blackout branches remain authoritative.

Suggested implementation order: (a) four-slot identities and ordinary doubles using original fixtures; (b) targets, protocol, HUD and replacements; (c) four-owner AI-partner battles and partner-wild capture/run; (d) asymmetric trainer pairs and ownership-sensitive moves; (e) VM/world wiring and story replays. Keep each intermediate topology explicitly unsupported until its acceptance cases pass.

VM work must replace the logged-win branch in `trainerBattle` and `start_tag_battle`, preserving equal/distinct trainer IDs and passing the explicit partner argument. World work must construct two partner-wild opponents from encounter rolls and carry the persistent partner context. The existing VM discards the explicit tag partner and wins the encounter immediately, so changing only `controller.ts` cannot make story doubles real. [Current VM][L-vm], decomp entry evidence §1.

Importer work is conditional: reuse existing `Trainer.double`, `Trainer.ai`, class and party records and the already imported AI script. If adding target-menu art, doubles HUD assets, message records or layout metadata changes importer output, bump `IMPORTER_VERSION` in `packages/app/src/importer.ts` in the implementation PR. This research PR changes no importer output. [Importer rule](../scope.md), [current trainer double check][L-vm], [current range mapping and format][L-dex].
